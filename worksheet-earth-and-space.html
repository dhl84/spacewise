Curriculum (England, Year 5 Earth and space):

  • the movement of the Earth and the other planets relative to the Sun,
  • the movement of the Moon relative to the Earth,
  • the Sun, Earth and Moon as approximately spherical bodies,
  • day and night.

Answers for the teacher

  1. It depends on the time of the lesson. In daytime in the UK, the pin is on the sunny half.
  2. The Earth turns. The Sun only seems to move across our sky.
  3. "... the Earth spins (rotates), so each place turns into the sunlight and then away from it."
  4. 24 hours. (The Earth turns once relative to the stars in 23 hours 56 minutes. A full day from noon to noon is 24 hours.)
  5. Approximately spherical (round like a ball).
  6. About 4 (6,371 ÷ 1,737 is about 3.7).
  7. Bigger. The Sun's radius is about 109 times the Earth's (695,700 ÷ 6,371).
  8. The Sun.
  9. The Earth.
  10. Moon B (27.3 days), Earth C (365.26 days), Mars A (687 days).
  11. Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
  12. With time sped up, the stars and the Sun all seem to move together because the Earth turns. From space you see the Earth spin and the line between day and night move.
